>I do not know what I may appear to the world, but to myself I seem to have been only like a boy playing on the sea-shore, and diverting myself in now and then finding a smoother pebble or a prettier shell than ordinary, whilst the great ocean of truth lay all undiscovered before me.

- Memoirs of the Life, Writings, and Discoveries of Sir Isaac Newton_ (1855) by Sir David Brewster (Volume II. Ch. 27). Compare: "As children gath'ring pebbles on the shore", [John Milton](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/John_Milton "John Milton"), _Paradise Regained_, Book iv. Line 330

>Rather accuse him under usual names,  
  Fortune and Fate, as one regardless quite

  True wisdom finds her not, or, by delusion  
  Far worse, her false resemblance only meets,       
  An empty cloud. However, many books,  
  Wise men have said, are wearisome; who reads  
  Incessantly, and to his reading brings not  
  A spirit and judgment equal or superior,  
 () And what he brings what needs he elsewhere seek?)  
  Uncertain and unsettled still remains,  
  Deep-versed in books and shallow in himself,  
  Crude or intoxicate, collecting toys
